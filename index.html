Research

IPO Spillovers: Evidence from Private-Firm Networks

Job Market Paper

Abstract

This paper studies how IPO decisions propagate through firm networks formed by common investors. Using a dynamic network constructed from Crunchbase data for 2000--2023, I show that firms are more likely to go public after directly connected peers complete IPOs. These spillovers are stronger when firms share a lead investor, but weaker when their peers are already exposed to IPO activity through more distant network connections. Peer IPOs also predict higher valuations, share prices, and subsequent funding outcomes. I develop a dynamic learning model in which IPOs reveal information about common investors' ability to take firms successfully to public markets. This information raises connected firms' expected public-market valuations and their incentives to pursue an IPO. The model rationalizes the positive first-order spillovers, their amplification through lead investors, and their attenuation with second-order IPO exposure.
