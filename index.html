Research

IPO Spillovers: Evidence from Private-Firm Networks

Job Market Paper

Abstract

I study how IPO decisions propagate through firm networks formed by common investors. Using a dynamic network constructed from Crunchbase data for 2000–2023, I show that firms are more likely to go public after directly connected peers complete IPOs. These spillovers are stronger when firms share a lead investor, but weaker when their peers are already exposed to IPO activity through more distant network connections. Peer IPOs also predict higher valuations, share prices, and subsequent funding outcomes. I develop a dynamic learning model in which IPOs reveal information about common investors' ability to take firms successfully to public markets. This information raises connected firms' expected public-market valuations and their incentives to pursue an IPO. The model rationalizes the positive first-order spillovers, their amplification through lead investors, and their attenuation with second-order IPO exposure.

Internal Migration Choices across Households when Insuring Income Risk

Abstract

I study how informal risk-sharing networks shape households’ incentives to undertake risky internal migration when migration income itself may be subject to sharing. I develop a network model in which households face uncertain rural income, choose whether to undertake a costly migration opportunity with stochastic returns, and share resources with connected households. Social connections therefore provide insurance against income risk while also reducing the private return to migration through kinship taxation. Under CARA preferences, background resources factor out of the migration decision, so neighbors’ migration choices do not generate strategic interaction. Under DARA preferences, instead, resources received through the network affect households’ willingness to bear migration risk. When remittances are shared, a neighbor’s migration increases household resources and lowers the migration threshold, generating strategic complementarity in migration choices. Finally, forming an additional risk-sharing link has an ambiguous effect, reflecting the trade-off between additional resources and greater sharing obligations.
